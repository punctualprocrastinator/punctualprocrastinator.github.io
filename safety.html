Known by Construction: Calibrating Model-State Instruments Against Models Whose State Was Installed, Hidden, or Never Present

NeurIPS 2026 · New in ML

Manan Wadhwa*, Shivam Dubey*

People increasingly ask whether an AI system has hidden goals, preferences or something like feelings. The tools for checking are mostly tested by seeing whether they agree with each other, and never against a case where the true answer is known. We built small AI models where we controlled the answer so that some were trained to have a hidden habit, and some were not. None of the tools we tested could reliably tell those models apart, signaling that agreement between tools is not a reliable verdict.

Dead Salmons Have a False-Positive Rate: A Calibrated Null-Model Test for Interpretability

NeurIPS 2026 · Interpretability as a Science

Manan Wadhwa*, Shivam Dubey*

When we look inside a model to see what it has learned, say if it has picked up something like sentiment, we use methods like linear probes, SAEs and concept directions. This paper runs those methods on untrained models, which have learned nothing at all, and finds they report the same confident results. The proposed fix is simple: check every finding against an untrained baseline before trusting it.

Steered Affect Moves a Partner Agent’s Activations, and the Logistic Probe That Reads It Returns a Tie-Break, Not an Estimate

NeurIPS 2026 · Interpreting Agent Behavior

Manan Wadhwa*, Shivam Dubey*, Debojeet Ghosh, Shreyansh Kumar, Ayushman Garg

As AI agents become progressively more talkative around each other, it matters whether one agent’s emotional tone can carry over into another. We pushed one AI agent into an emotional state such as sadness or fear, and found measurable changes inside the agent it was talking to. We also found a common method for detecting these changes gives different answers when run twice on similar data. Once we accounted for that, only the effect for sadness held up clearly. The broader point is that some standard tools for inspecting AI systems need stronger checks before people rely on their results.

Toxin Circuits in ESM-2: Mechanistic Interpretability for Biosecurity Screening

ICML 2026 · GenBio Workshop

Manan Wadhwa*, Shivam Dubey*

BLAST detects 0% of 643 ProteinMPNN redesigns; a frozen ESM-2 linear probe detects 93.9% with no retraining. SAE analysis shows why: redesign amplifies toxin structural features rather than obscuring them. A four-tier attack taxonomy places the security boundary at gradient access (6.1% evasion black-box against 100% white-box), and direct probe attribution pinpoints Layer 32 as the detection bottleneck (r = 0.992).

Isolating Refusal and Compliance Heads: Unconditional Bipolar Defenses

2026 · safety circuits, Qwen2.5-7B

Shivam Dubey

A compliance-score classifier reaches AUC 0.943 (Cohen's d = 2.636) with zero false positives across five benign categories: a working zero-shot white-box harm detector. Unconditional bipolar assertion, amplifying refusal heads while zero-ablating compliance heads at L25-H4, holds attack success to 33% under nanoGCG against 36–42% for conditional gating and 66% undefended: the precision of conditional gating is itself a structural vulnerability under white-box optimization. Layer 25 shows extreme intra-layer antagonism between the two head types.

Pre-commitment Probing in Open-Weight Reasoning Models

2026 · OLMo-3-7B-Think

Shivam Dubey

A linear probe on the residual stream predicts answer correctness before any reasoning token is emitted (AUC 0.780, 5-fold CV). The model commits before it reasons, which makes the chain of thought post-hoc rationalization rather than deliberation. A functional frustration direction at Layer 15 transfers cross-lingually across six languages (p = 0.0017): a sub-linguistic computational state invisible in the output but readable mechanistically, usable either as a monitor or as an attack surface.

White-box monitoring under adaptive attack · MARS V

Compliance. We train linear probes on residual-stream activations across four instruction-tuned models from three labs (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, abliterated Llama-3). Compliance is linearly decodable. The probe tracks refusal itself rather than surface phrasing; length-matched accuracy is indistinguishable across models.

We then ran a red-team/blue-team co-evolution loop against those probes across three attack families (suffix-gradient, discrete search, activation steering). Jailbreak success and probe evasion came out mutually exclusive: the perturbations that evaded the monitor also stopped the jailbreak from working. The harness that runs it is Whetstone, which is open source.

What the Failure Mode Is Not. The result is not that linear probes fail to find the jailbreak direction. They find it: compliance is linearly decodable in all four models. The problem is the false positive rate. Every probe has one, including the best of them, and at deployment volume a small rate is a large number of false alarms. A deployed system therefore needs a proper monitor cascade, not one probe and not a handful of them.

Next. We are extending this into trained attack policies (Redwood’s open problem on stress-testing white-box monitors), gated behind a six-stage empirical de-risking protocol. One confound dominates the design: whether the probe reads the model’s behavior or merely the instructions it was given. The ablation that separates them is written.
