Five workshop papers in one cycle

13 October 2026

Three at NeurIPS with Manan — New in ML, Interpretability as a Science, Interpreting Agent Behavior — and two at ATTRIB from the Lexsi side. All non-archival, which people sometimes read as a lesser thing. It is not. Workshops are where you find out whether the result survives contact with people who already know the area.

The one I am most pleased with is Known by Construction, because it is the least impressive-sounding. We built models where we already knew the answer, ran the instruments at them, and none of them could tell the models apart. That is a negative result about tools everybody is currently trusting.

Whetstone is public

10 October 2026

The harness behind the MARS co-evolution experiments is up, with docs. Red team, blue team, a monitor in the middle, and enough structure that you can swap any of the three without rewriting the other two.

Most of the work was not the attacks. It was making runs comparable to each other — seeds, logging, a shared evaluation path — so that when a number moves you know whether it moved for a reason.

The result I wanted was not there

20 September 2026

At MARS I spent a while trying to break linear compliance probes with adaptive attackers: suffix-gradient, discrete search, activation steering. The expectation was that a determined attacker evades the monitor and jailbreaks the model at the same time. That is the scenario that makes white-box monitoring look fragile.

It did not happen. Across all three families, the perturbations that evaded the probe also stopped the jailbreak from working — the two were mutually exclusive. I have been careful about how I say this, because it is easy to over-read. It constrains a narrow class of attacks. It is not a guarantee, and the next step, trained attack policies, is exactly where the constraint might stop holding.

Teaching without the libraries

15 August 2026

Redarc Fellows runs on PyTorch hooks and Hugging Face, with no interpretability library in the stack. People ask why, usually politely.

Because the libraries are good, and that is the problem for eight weeks. If your first contact with a residual stream is a function that returns it to you, you learn the function. If you have to reach in and take it, you learn where it is. After that, use whatever you like — the curriculum is only eight sessions, and the point of them is to make the abstractions legible rather than load-bearing.
