Bio

I work on mechanistic interpretability and adversarial robustness. Right now that mostly means white-box monitoring for AI control: building monitors that read a model's activations, and then attacking them with adaptive attackers to find out what they are really measuring.

I am a fellow on the Lindley Lentati track at MARS V in Cambridge, a research fellow at Apart Research, and co-founder of Redarc Labs, a small AI safety research fellowship in Delhi. I also do capability research on tabular foundation models at Lexsi Labs. I study data science at IIT Madras.

The order I try to work in is understand, exploit, then defend. A defence that has not survived someone optimising against it is a hypothesis, not a result — so most of my projects end with me trying to break the thing I just built. Sometimes that works and the headline result is negative. I would rather publish those than not.

London, 2026.

More about me

I am doing two degrees at once — a BS in data science at IIT Madras and a B.Tech in mechanical and automation engineering in Delhi — which was a worse idea than it sounded in 2022 and a better one than it felt in 2024.

I teach the Redarc Fellows cohort, eight weeks of mechanistic interpretability written with PyTorch hooks and Hugging Face directly, no wrapper libraries. People learn the thing faster when nothing is hidden from them. Most of what I know I learned by reimplementing somebody's result and watching it not reproduce.

If you are an undergraduate wondering whether you can do interpretability research without a PhD or a lab: email me. The answer is yes and the first step is smaller than it looks.
